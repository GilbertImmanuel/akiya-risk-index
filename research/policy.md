## 2. The 2015 Act: `特定空家等`

The 2015 framework targets **`特定空家等`** — vacant houses that are, or are likely to become, a serious danger to safety, sanitation, landscape, or the living environment. The statutory definition covers four situations:

1. Leaving the house standing risks collapse or other serious danger to safety.
2. Leaving it standing risks serious harm to sanitation.
3. Inadequate management significantly damages the landscape.
4. Leaving it standing is otherwise inappropriate for preserving the surrounding living environment.

The process is:

> **助言・指導 → 勧告 → 命令 → 行政代執行**

A **勧告** (recommendation) is the key step for tax purposes: the site is then excluded from the residential-land tax exception. An **命令** (order) and **行政代執行** (administrative execution) are stronger enforcement steps.

**Fact.** The 2023 amendment added a `略式代執行` route for cases where the owner cannot be identified, and the 2025 enforcement survey reports its use.

## 3. The 2023 amendment: `管理不全空家等`

The 2023 amendment created **`管理不全空家等`**, a category *before* `特定空家等`:

> A vacant house that, because of inadequate management, is at risk of becoming a `特定空家等` if left as it is.

The process is:

> **指導 → 勧告**

A municipality can issue **guidance** (`指導`) to the owner. If the condition is not improved and the risk of becoming a `特定空家等` is significant, the municipality can issue a **recommendation** (`勧告`). After a recommendation, the site is excluded from the residential-land tax exception, just like a `特定空家等` after a recommendation.

**Important distinction.** The brief said the 2023 Act allows municipalities to "designate" poorly managed vacant houses. In the statute, this is a **determination plus guidance/recommendation process**, not a separate formal "designation" in the same sense as a zoning designation. Municipalities may use the word "designation" in local practice, but the legal trigger for the tax consequence is the **勧告**.

The 2023 amendment also introduced or strengthened:

- **空家等管理活用支援法人** — designated support corporations that can manage, use, or dispose of vacant houses.
- **空家等活用促進区域** — zones where redevelopment and use promotion are easier.
- **略式代執行** — simplified administrative execution where the owner cannot be identified.
- Updated national guidelines and basic policy.

**Fact.** The 2023 amendment took effect on **13 December 2023**. It was not a 2026 change.

## 4. Tax treatment: what actually happens

The residential-land tax exception (`住宅用地特例`) under the Local Tax Act reduces the fixed-asset tax base:

| Land area | Fixed-asset tax base | Urban-planning tax base |
|---|---:|---:|
| ≤200 m² | **1/6** of value | 1/3 of value |
| >200 m² | **1/3** of value | 2/3 of value |

For a `特定空家等` or `管理不全空家等` that has received a **勧告**, the site is **excluded from the exception**. The taxable value can revert to the full value.

**Inference.** For a small residential lot (≤200 m²), the taxable value can rise by up to **6x** (from 1/6 to 1). For a larger lot, the increase is up to **3x** (from 1/3 to 1). This is a statement about the **tax base**, not necessarily the final tax bill: burden-adjustment measures, municipal ordinances, and assessment practices can reduce the actual increase. The common phrase "the tax becomes 6x" is therefore an oversimplification.

**Fact.** The 2015 implementation guidance also says the exception may not apply where the house is not expected to be used for residential purposes — for example, where it is structurally not a dwelling, is scheduled for demolition, or is not being managed for residential use. This means some owners can lose the exception **without** a formal recommendation. This nuance comes from a law-firm commentary and the MLIT tax overview; confirm the exact statutory basis in Phase 0.

**Timing.** Fixed-asset tax is assessed on the **1 January** base date. The practical effect of a recommendation therefore depends on when it is issued relative to 1 January. The project should not assume an immediate same-year change; verify the transition in Phase 0.

## 5. The 2026 registration obligation

This is a **registration reform**, not a vacancy tax.

- **Start:** 1 April 2026.
- **Obligation:** If an owner's address or name changes, the change must be registered within **2 years**.
- **Transition:** Changes that occurred before 1 April 2026 must be registered by **31 March 2028**.
- **Penalty:** Up to **¥50,000** for a violation without a legitimate reason.
- **Procedure:** A free "smart change registration" route is available; the legal registration is then handled by the Legal Affairs Bureau.

**Why it matters for vacancy:** accurate owner addresses make it easier for municipalities to contact absentee owners and to identify who is responsible for a vacant house. It does **not** create a tax, and it does **not** by itself force a sale or occupation.

The **2024 inheritance-registration obligation** is a separate reform with the same broad goal: making ownership visible and reducing unknown-owner properties. It is not a vacancy measure either, but it affects the ownership data environment.

## 6. National vs local tax proposals

There is **no national akiya tax** as of 2026-11-12.

### DPFP `空室税` bill

- Submitted by the Democratic Party for the People (国民民主党) on **11 December 2025** as a private member's bill.
- Aimed at **non-residential/investment housing in urban areas**, where prices and rents are rising, not at rural akiya.
- Would require the government to establish a system; it is not a detailed tax design.
- **Not law.** The DPFP's 2026 policy platform still lists it as a proposal.

### Kyoto City `非居住住宅利活用促進税`

- A **local statutory tax** on non-residential housing in Kyoto's urbanisation area.
- Originally planned for FY2026, then FY2029, now **FY2030**; the official page (updated 21 August 2026) cites system development for the one-year delay.
- Tax base: building value plus a location/floor-area component.
- Rates: 0.7% on building value; 0.15%–0.6% on the location/floor-area component, depending on value.
- Exemptions: business use, rental/sale intent, historic buildings, and certain temporary absences.
- This is a **Kyoto-specific local tax**, not a national model.

### 2026 tax reform

The 2026 tax reform outline continues existing vacant-house and land measures. It does **not** introduce a national vacancy tax. Do not describe the DPFP bill or the Kyoto tax as a national "2026 akiya tax."

## 7. Enforcement statistics (as of 31 March 2025)

The MLIT survey covers 1,741 municipalities.

| Indicator | FY2023 | FY2024 | Cumulative / stock |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `管理不全空家等` guidance | 666 | 2,545 | **3,211 (185 municipalities)** |
| `管理不全空家等` recommendations | 0 | 378 | **378 (40 municipalities)** |
| `特定空家等` advice/guidance | — | 4,026 | 42,768 (875 municipalities) |
| `特定空家等` recommendations | — | 600 | 4,153 (495 municipalities) |
| `特定空家等` orders | — | 101 | 551 (237 municipalities) |
| Administrative execution | — | 71 | 286 (175 municipalities) |
| Simplified execution | — | 81 | 592 (296 municipalities) |
| Emergency execution | — | 9 | 12 (10 municipalities) |

Other policy-capacity indicators:

- **1,541 municipalities (88.5%)** have a vacant-house plan.
- **95 support corporations** are designated in **64 municipalities**; 120 more are under consideration in 98 municipalities.
- **4 promotion zones** are designated in 4 cities; 36 more are under consideration in 27 municipalities.

**Fact.** The `管理不全` regime had no recommendations in its first year and 378 in its second. This is a rapid ramp-up, but the absolute number is still small relative to 3.856 million "other vacant" dwellings.

**Inference.** Designation counts measure **enforcement activity**, not the underlying stock of blight. A municipality with a well-staffed program may report more designations than a similarly blighted municipality with no capacity. Do not use designations as an unbiased risk measure.

## 9. Implications for the index

- **Do not treat the 2023 Act as a clean natural experiment.** It applies nationally, implementation is staggered by municipal capacity, and designation is endogenous. A causal evaluation is out of scope.
- **Use designations as descriptive context, not validation.** Prefecture-level counts and case studies are appropriate; municipal counts are not published.
- **Distinguish the tax base from the tax bill.** If the project discusses fiscal risk, state the 1/6 → 1 (or 1/3 → 1) mechanism and the burden-adjustment caveat.
- **Track the 2026 registration reform as a data-quality change.** Over time, owner-address data may improve; do not assume an immediate effect on vacancy.
- **Do not include a “national akiya tax” scenario.** It does not exist in law.
